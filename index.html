💡 What is an Inverter?
An inverter converts DC power (from a battery or solar panel) to AC power that runs your appliances. If your total wattage exceeds the inverter's rating, it will shut down or get damaged.
✅ Keep your load at 80% or less of inverter capacity for safe, long-term operation.

⚡ Learn Electricity
Beginner-friendly lessons on how electricity works
⚡ What is a Watt?
▼
A Watt (W) measures how much power an appliance uses right now. Think of it as the speed at which energy is consumed.

💡 A 60W bulb uses 60 joules of energy every second.
The higher the watts, the more electricity the device consumes. Your electricity bill depends on how many watts you use, for how long.
🔌 What is a Volt?
▼
A Volt (V) is the "pressure" that pushes electricity through a wire — like water pressure in a pipe.

🇵🇭 The Philippines uses 220 Volts as the standard home voltage.
Higher voltage means more electrical "push." That's why high-voltage lines are dangerous — the pressure is enormous.
💧 What is an Ampere?
▼
An Ampere (A) measures the flow of electric current — how many electrons pass through a wire per second. Like the width of a water pipe.

🔗 Watts = Volts × Amperes (P = V × I)
A device that draws 2 amps at 220 volts uses 440 watts of power.
📊 How does kWh work?
▼
kWh (kilowatt-hour) is what your electric meter measures. 1 kWh = using 1,000 watts for 1 hour.

Formula: kWh = (Watts × Hours) ÷ 1000
Example: A 100W fan running for 8 hours = 0.8 kWh.

Your bill = Total kWh × Price per kWh (in the Philippines, roughly 10–12/kWh depending on your area and provider).
💸 How is the electricity bill computed?
▼
Your electricity bill comes from your local utility company (like CEPALCO in CDO). They measure how many kWh you consumed for the month.

Bill = Total kWh × Rate (/kWh) + Fixed Charges
Fixed charges include: distribution, transmission, system loss, taxes. The actual rate printed on your bill is the "all-in" rate.

💡 Tip: Reduce kWh by turning off devices when not in use, using inverter-type appliances, and switching to LED lights.
⚠️ What causes circuit overload?
▼
An overload happens when too many appliances draw more current than your wiring or circuit breaker can handle. This causes wires to heat up — a fire hazard!

🏠 Typical household circuit breaker: 15A–30A (3,300W–6,600W)
Signs of overload: tripped breakers, flickering lights, warm outlets. Never bypass a circuit breaker!
